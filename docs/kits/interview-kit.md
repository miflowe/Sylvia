# Sylvia customer interview kit

The Imagine Cup deck must show **how we validated our assumptions with real customers**. Today we have no evidence.
This kit gets us real quotes, numbers and "would you pay?" answers before the deck is written (December).

**Owner:** the team. **Start:** this week. **Target by Dec 6:** 25+ conversations, logged in the findings sheet.

## 1. What we need to learn (hypotheses)
Each conversation tests some of these. Mark each one ✅ supported, ❌ contradicted, or ❓ unclear in the findings sheet.

| # | Hypothesis | Who tells us |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Residents near forest or bush don't know their local fire danger or air quality day to day. | Residents |
| H2 | People sensitive to smoke want earlier, more personal warnings than broadcast alerts give them. | Smoke-sensitive residents, caregivers |
| H3 | Homeowners can't tell invasive or fire-prone plants from safe ones, and would swap if shown a good native alternative. | Residents, gardeners |
| H4 | A free app that explains "what this means for you" would be used weekly in fire season. | Residents |
| H5 | Nurseries want customers sent to them for native, lower-flammability plants, and would pay referral or listing fees. | Nurseries, garden centres |
| H6 | Towns and regional districts would pay for a dashboard showing resident engagement (sightings, scans, checklist completion) to support FireSmart and invasive-plant programs. | Municipal staff, fire / emergency managers |
| H7 | Official alerts (Alert Ready, BC Wildfire Service) feel late or too broad to residents. | Residents |

## 2. Who to talk to (targets)
Include Ontario **and** BC in every group. Imagine Cup scores diversity and inclusion, so deliberately include older
adults, newcomers, rural residents, people with disabilities, and Indigenous communities (approach through community
organizations and follow their protocols; never cold-call).

| Segment | Target | Where to find them |
|---|---|---|
| Residents near forest or bush (cottage country, northern ON, BC interior) | 10 | Family and friends, cottage associations, community Facebook groups, r/ontario and r/britishcolumbia |
| Smoke-sensitive people (asthma, COPD, heart conditions, older adults, parents of young kids) and caregivers | 6 | Asthma Canada and lung health communities, seniors' centres, parent groups |
| Gardeners and homeowners who landscape | 4 | Horticultural societies, community gardens, master gardeners |
| Nurseries and garden centres (try Grow Me Instead / FireSmart-recognized ones) | 4 | Grow Me Instead nursery list, FireSmart BC Plant Program retailers |
| Town / regional district staff (FireSmart coordinators, emergency managers, parks) | 3 | FireSmart BC local coordinators, Ontario municipal emergency management offices |
| Experts (Invasive Species Council of BC, Ontario Invasive Plant Council, FireSmart Canada) | 2 | Their public contact pages |

## 3. Outreach message (adapt per person)
> Hi [name], I'm [your name], a student at Toronto Metropolitan University. Our team is building Sylvia, a free app
> that helps people near forests understand wildfire smoke and fire risk, and choose native plants that are less likely
> to feed a fire. We're entering Microsoft's Imagine Cup and want to learn from people who live this. Could I ask you
> about 20 minutes of questions, by phone, video or in person? No sales pitch, and your answers stay anonymous.

## 4. Before every interview
- Ask permission to take notes, and separately ask before recording anything. No recording without a clear yes.
- Say: "There are no wrong answers, and you can skip any question or stop at any time."
- Never collect health details beyond what they volunteer. Never write down full names next to health information.
- Ask a faculty advisor whether anything you plan needs TMU research-ethics review. Product interviews usually don't,
  but publishing results might.

## 5. Interview guides (~20 min each)
Ask about **past behaviour**, not opinions about our idea. Show the app only at the end, if at all.

### Residents (incl. smoke-sensitive)
1. Tell me about the last time wildfire smoke or a nearby fire affected you. What happened, and what did you do?
2. How did you first find out? How long after it started?
3. Where do you check fire or air-quality information today? How often in summer?
4. What was missing or confusing in that information?
5. *(If smoke-sensitive or a caregiver)* How does smoke affect you or the person you care for? At what point do you change plans: close windows, stay in, leave?
6. Have you ever received an official emergency alert (Alert Ready, BC Wildfire Service)? Was it early enough? Specific enough?
7. What's in your yard? Have you ever wondered whether a plant was invasive or a fire risk? What did you do?
8. If an app told you a plant in your yard was invasive or highly flammable and suggested a native swap, what would make you actually replace it?
9. What would make you trust or distrust an app like this?
10. *(End)* Show the Sylvia screens. "What's the first thing you'd use? What would you never use?"

### Nurseries and garden centres
1. How often do customers ask about native, invasive, or fire-resistant plants?
2. Do you take part in Grow Me Instead, FireSmart, or similar programs? What do you get out of them?
3. How do new customers find you today? What does a new customer cost you?
4. If an app sent you customers looking for a specific native plant, how would you want that to work?
5. **Would you pay for that?** Per referral, a monthly listing, or neither? Roughly how much would feel fair?
6. Would you put a QR code or tag on plants that links to the app?

### Town / regional district staff
1. What wildfire-prevention or invasive-plant programs do you run? How do you measure whether residents engage?
2. How do you reach residents before and during fire season today? What works? What doesn't?
3. What would you want to see about your residents' activity (anonymized): sightings, plant swaps, checklist completion?
4. **Would your budget pay for that?** Which budget line, roughly how much per year, and who decides?
5. What would stop you adopting a tool like this (privacy, procurement, liability, IT approval)?

### Experts
1. Which invasive plants are the biggest problem in [ON/BC], and which are also fire hazards?
2. Which sources should we trust for invasive status and flammability ratings?
3. What claims should a public app never make?
4. Would you review our plant data or recommendations?

## 6. Short survey (for reach beyond interviews)
Build in **Microsoft Forms** (it helps the Microsoft story). Keep it to 3 minutes:
1. Province / territory and community type (city, suburb, small town, rural, cottage).
2. Have you been affected by wildfire smoke in the last 3 years? (never / once / several times / every year)
3. Are you or someone you care for sensitive to smoke? (yes / no / prefer not to say)
4. How do you currently get wildfire or air-quality information? (multi-select)
5. How early did you hear about the last fire or smoke event near you? (before it reached me / as it arrived / after / never)
6. Do you know if any plants in your yard are invasive? (yes / no / not sure)
7. Would you use a free app that explains your local fire and smoke risk and suggests safer native plants? (1–5)
8. What would make you trust it? (open text)
9. Optional email to test the app (store separately from answers).

## 7. Findings sheet (copy into Excel / SharePoint)
| Date | Interviewer | Segment | Province | Community type | Key quote (verbatim) | Pain observed | Current workaround | H# tested | Result (✅/❌/❓) | SDG target | Would pay? (B2B: amount) | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**SDG target:** the UN Sustainable Development Goal target a finding backs up, if any: 3.9 (smoke and health),
11.5 (warnings that reduce harm from disasters), 13.1 (preparing for climate hazards like longer fire seasons) or
15.8 (invasive plants). Leave it blank when none fits; never stretch one.

After every 5 interviews, the team spends 15 minutes on: which hypotheses are holding, what surprised us, and what
we change in the app. Send me the sheet and I'll turn the patterns into deck slides and product changes.

## 8. What goes in the deck
- "We spoke to N people across Ontario and BC": a segment breakdown chart.
- 3–4 verbatim quotes, one per pain point.
- Hypothesis scorecard (H1–H7).
- Willingness-to-pay evidence from nurseries and towns (the business model slide).
- SDG evidence: one real finding behind each of the four UN goal targets on the roadmap slide.
- What we changed because of what we heard (judges love this).
