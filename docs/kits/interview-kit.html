What we need to learn

Seven assumptions to test

After each conversation, mark the ones it touched as supported, contradicted or unclear in the findings sheet.

  1. H1Residents near forest or bush don't know their local fire danger or air quality day to day.Residents
  2. H2People sensitive to smoke want earlier, more personal warnings than broadcast alerts give them.Smoke-sensitive residents, caregivers
  3. H3Homeowners can't tell invasive or fire-prone plants from safe ones, and would swap if shown a good native alternative.Residents, gardeners
  4. H4A free app that explains what the numbers mean for you would be used weekly in fire season.Residents
  5. H5Nurseries want customers sent to them for native, lower-flammability plants, and would pay referral or listing fees.Nurseries, garden centres
  6. H6Towns and regional districts would pay for a dashboard of resident engagement (sightings, scans, checklist completion) to support FireSmart and invasive-plant programs.Municipal staff, fire and emergency managers
  7. H7Official alerts (Alert Ready, BC Wildfire Service) feel late or too broad to residents.Residents

Who to talk to

29 conversations across six groups

Residents near forest or bush

10

Cottage country, northern Ontario, BC interior. Family and friends, cottage associations, community Facebook groups, r/ontario and r/britishcolumbia.

People sensitive to smoke, and caregivers

6

Asthma, COPD, heart conditions, older adults, parents of young kids. Lung-health communities, seniors' centres, parent groups.

Experts

2

Invasive Species Council of BC, Ontario Invasive Plant Council, FireSmart Canada.

Include Ontario and BC in every group. Imagine Cup scores diversity and inclusion, so deliberately include older adults, newcomers, rural residents and people with disabilities. Reach Indigenous communities through community organizations and follow their protocols. Never cold-call.

Reaching out

Outreach message

Hi [name], I'm [your name], a student at Toronto Metropolitan University. Our team is building Sylvia, a free app that helps people near forests understand wildfire smoke and fire risk, and choose native plants that are less likely to feed a fire. We're entering Microsoft's Imagine Cup and want to learn from people who live this. Could I ask you about 20 minutes of questions, by phone, video or in person? No sales pitch, and your answers stay anonymous.

Before every interview

Ground rules

  • Ask permission to take notes. Ask separately before recording anything; no recording without a clear yes.
  • Say: "There are no wrong answers, and you can skip any question or stop at any time."
  • Don't collect health details beyond what people volunteer, and never write a full name next to health information.
  • Ask about what people did in the past, not what they think of our idea. Show the app only at the end, if at all.
  • Check with a faculty advisor whether anything needs TMU research-ethics review. Product interviews usually don't; publishing results might.

Interview guides

Questions by group

Includes smoke-sensitive people and caregivers. Tests H1, H2, H3, H4, H7.

  1. Tell me about the last time wildfire smoke or a nearby fire affected you. What happened, and what did you do?
  2. How did you first find out? How long after it started?
  3. Where do you check fire or air-quality information today? How often in summer?
  4. What was missing or confusing in that information?
  5. If smoke-sensitive or a caregiver: how does smoke affect you or the person you care for? At what point do you change plans: close windows, stay in, leave?
  6. Have you ever received an official emergency alert (Alert Ready, BC Wildfire Service)? Was it early enough? Specific enough?
  7. What's in your yard? Have you ever wondered whether a plant was invasive or a fire risk? What did you do?
  8. If an app told you a plant in your yard was invasive or highly flammable and suggested a native swap, what would make you actually replace it?
  9. What would make you trust or distrust an app like this?
  10. At the end, show the Sylvia screens: what's the first thing you'd use? What would you never use?

Tests H5. The highlighted question is the one the business model slide depends on.

  1. How often do customers ask about native, invasive or fire-resistant plants?
  2. Do you take part in Grow Me Instead, FireSmart or similar programs? What do you get out of them?
  3. How do new customers find you today? What does a new customer cost you?
  4. If an app sent you customers looking for a specific native plant, how would you want that to work?
  5. Would you pay for that? Per referral, a monthly listing, or neither? Roughly how much would feel fair?
  6. Would you put a QR code or tag on plants that links to the app?

Tests H6. Bring screenshots of the Communities view if it's ready.

  1. What wildfire-prevention or invasive-plant programs do you run? How do you measure whether residents engage?
  2. How do you reach residents before and during fire season today? What works? What doesn't?
  3. What would you want to see about your residents' activity, anonymized: sightings, plant swaps, checklist completion?
  4. Would your budget pay for that? Which budget line, roughly how much per year, and who decides?
  5. What would stop you adopting a tool like this: privacy, procurement, liability, IT approval?

Credibility for the plant data. Ask whether we may name them in the deck.

  1. Which invasive plants are the biggest problem in Ontario or BC, and which are also fire hazards?
  2. Which sources should we trust for invasive status and flammability ratings?
  3. What claims should a public app never make?
  4. Would you review our plant data or recommendations?

For reach beyond interviews

Three-minute survey

Build it in Microsoft Forms; that also helps the Microsoft story. Copy the questions below into a new form.

  1. Province or territory, and community type City, suburb, small town, rural, cottage
  2. Have you been affected by wildfire smoke in the last 3 years? Never · once · several times · every year
  3. Are you, or someone you care for, sensitive to smoke? Yes · no · prefer not to say
  4. How do you get wildfire or air-quality information today? Choose all that apply
  5. How early did you hear about the last fire or smoke event near you? Before it reached me · as it arrived · after · never
  6. Do you know if any plants in your yard are invasive? Yes · no · not sure
  7. Would you use a free app that explains your local fire and smoke risk and suggests safer native plants? 1 (no) to 5 (definitely)
  8. What would make you trust it? Open text
  9. Optional: your email to test the app Store it separately from the answers
